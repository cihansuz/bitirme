# ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes - 2024: Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT)

## ADA_2024_P14: Carbohydrate Intake and Quality
In individuals with type 2 diabetes, carbohydrate intake should emphasize nutrient-dense carbohydrate sources with high dietary fiber and minimal added sugars. Monitoring carbohydrate intake and considering the blood glucose response to dietary carbohydrates are key to improving postprandial glucose control. Diets should prioritize whole grains, legumes, vegetables, and whole fruits over refined carbohydrates and sugar-sweetened beverages.

## ADA_2024_P15: Fiber Targets and Glycemic Control
Evidence confirms that higher dietary fiber intake—specifically reaching or exceeding 30 g/day (or 14 g per 1000 kcal)—is associated with lower all-cause mortality, reduced glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), improved insulin sensitivity, and reduced glycemic excursions in patients with type 2 diabetes. Soluble fiber slows gastric emptying and carbohydrate absorption, attenuating postprandial hyperglycemia.

## ADA_2024_P16: Diagnostic Cutoffs and Biomarker Interpretation
The American Diabetes Association defines diagnostic thresholds as follows:
- Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) indicates Diabetes Mellitus. Impaired Fasting Glucose (Prediabetes) is defined between 100 mg/dL and 125 mg/dL.
- Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c) >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) indicates Diabetes Mellitus. Prediabetes ranges from 5.7% to 6.4%.
- When HbA1c is above 8.0%, immediate dietary and pharmacotherapy reassessment is warranted to mitigate microvascular and macrovascular complications.

## ADA_2024_P17: Saturated Fat and Lipid Management
For individuals with diabetes and dyslipidemia, saturated fatty acid intake should be restricted to less than 7% to 10% of total daily energy intake, replacing saturated fats with monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA) and polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) from olive oil, nuts, seeds, and oily fish to mitigate atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk.

## ADA_2024_P18: Added Sugar and Sweetener Restrictions
Energy intake from free or added sugars should be minimized (ideally 0% added refined sugars). Sugar-sweetened beverages, commercial baked goods, and syrups promote acute postprandial glycemic spikes and hepatic steatosis, and are strongly discouraged.
