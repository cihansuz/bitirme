# Clinical Guidelines on Drug-Nutrient and Drug-Diet Interactions

## DRUG_INT_P31: Metformin and Vitamin B12 Depletion
Metformin is a first-line biguanide for type 2 diabetes. Prolonged metformin therapy (>= 2 years) is causally associated with reduced intestinal absorption of Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) due to calcium-dependent ileal membrane antagonism. Diets should ensure adequate vitamin B12 food sources, and routine annual serum B12 monitoring is recommended. Concomitant heavy alcohol ingestion with metformin strongly increases the risk of life-threatening lactic acidosis and is strictly contraindicated.

## DRUG_INT_P32: Levothyroxine and Absorption Interferences
Levothyroxine (T4) requires an acidic gastric environment for optimal absorption. Ingestion of calcium carbonate supplements, dairy products (milk, yogurt), dietary iron supplements, or unfermented soy products concurrently with levothyroxine markedly decreases drug bioavailability by up to 30-50%. Patients must schedule calcium-, iron-, and soy-rich foods at least 4 hours apart from their morning levothyroxine dose.

## DRUG_INT_P33: SGLT2 Inhibitors and Euglycemic DKA Risks
Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors (e.g., empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) promote urinary glucose excretion. In patients treated with SGLT2 inhibitors, very-low-carbohydrate or ketogenic diets (< 50 g/day) trigger rapid glycogen depletion, accelerated lipolysis, and severe euglycemic diabetic ketoasidoz (euDKA) despite near-normal serum glucose levels. Extreme carbohydrate restriction is strictly contraindicated.

## DRUG_INT_P34: ACE Inhibitors and Hyperkalemia Precaution
Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors (e.g., ramipril, enalapril) decrease aldosterone secretion, reducing urinary potassium elimination. Concomitant excessive intake of potassium-rich salt substitutes (potassium chloride, KCl) or high-dose potassium supplements creates a high risk of lethal hyperkalemia, especially in patients with mild-to-moderate renal insufficiency.
